**Lesson Spec Sheet: Lesson 1**  
**1\. Lesson Objective**

* **Content**: Students will understand the basic definition of AI as a pattern recognition system, learn digital safety rules, and plan the visual structure of a personal website on paper.  
* **Language**: Students will acquire basic website structure vocabulary and AI terminology, and use English to sort scenarios and brainstorm website ideas.  
* **AI-literacy**: Students will adopt the "Operator" mindset, understand that AI is a prediction machine (not magic or a human), and learn to protect personal data using a digital mask.

**2\. Target Vocabulary** *(Note: Designed with a low cognitive load for an unplugged lesson without computers; vocabulary is kept concise to focus on concepts)*

1. AI (Artificial Intelligence)  
2. Operator  
3. Pattern  
4. Data  
5. Prediction  
6. Digital mask  
7. Nickname  
8. Landing page  
9. Header  
10. Navigation bar  
11. Footer  
12. Prompt

**3\. Grammar Focus** Not specified for this lesson. *(Note: Artificial linguistic constraints are introduced from Lesson 2).*  
**4\. Timing (90 minutes total)**

* **00:00 – 00:10 (10 min)**: Introduction, The Operator Rule, and reading the Driving Question aloud.  
* **00:10 – 00:35 (25 min)**: "AI or Not AI" Card Sort Activity & Collective Definition.  
* **00:35 – 00:50 (15 min)**: AI Safety, Digital Mask, and Classroom Agreement.  
* **00:50 – 01:05 (15 min)**: Website Structure Vocabulary & Mind Mapping.  
* **01:05 – 01:20 (15 min)**: Introduction to Prompts & Teacher Modeling (I do).  
* **01:20 – 01:30 (10 min)**: Prompt Journal Brainstorming & Wrap-up.

**5\. Scenario**

* **Intro & Driving Question (10 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher**: "Welcome to our course\! You are going to build your own websites using **AI**. But remember our golden rule: You are the **operator**. AI is your co-pilot, not your captain."  
* **Teacher**: "Throughout our five lessons, we will be trying to answer one main question. Listen carefully: **How can I critically partner with Artificial Intelligence to build a digital portfolio that authentically represents who I am to the world?**"  
* **"AI or Not AI" Card Sort (25 min) \- Open Discussion:**  
* *Question Bank*: "Why do you think this is AI?", "Why do you think this is not AI?", "Does this use a simple timer or does it learn your choices?"  
* *Expected Child Answers*: "It is AI because it learns what videos I like", "It is not AI because it is just a fixed timer", "I don't know, maybe the camera recognizes my face?".  
* **Teacher Script (Definition)**: "Let's make a rule together. AI is a computer system that finds a **pattern** in **data**. It uses these patterns to make a **prediction**. Predictions get better as more data is added."  
* **Safety & Digital Mask (15 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher**: "We must protect our data. We use a **digital mask**. Never type your real name, your exact address, or your phone number into the AI.  
* For example, do not write 'My name is Kirill, I am 12'. Instead, write 'a 12-year-old student from a big city'. Now, sign this classroom agreement using your **nickname**, not your real name."  
* Example 2:  
* Bad prompt: "I study at School Number 15, my phone is 0987654321,  
* write an intro for me."  
* Good prompt: "A middle school student who likes learning about  
* technology, write a short intro."  
* Example 3:  
* Bad prompt: "My brother Ivan is annoying and we live right next to  
* the central park."  
* Good prompt: "A person with a big family who enjoys walking in the park."  
* **Website Structure & Mind Mapping (15 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher**: "Let's plan your website on paper. Every website starts with a main **landing page**. At the top, we have a **header** and a **navigation bar** to find different pages. At the bottom, we have a **footer**. Draw your sections now."  
* Учитель моделирует mind map на собственном примере: MY WEBSITE и 3–4 пустые ветки; дети выбирают своё содержание.

* **Prompt Introduction & Teacher Modeling (15 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher**: "To talk to AI, we use a prompt. **A prompt is a set of clear instructions you write to tell the AI exactly what to make or write for you.**"  
* **Teacher (Thinking aloud on projector)**: "If I write a bad recipe, I get bad food. A vague recipe is: *'Write about football.'* The AI will write a boring, long essay. A perfect recipe is: *'Act as a 12-year-old sports blogger. Write a short, exciting welcome message for my website about why I love playing football. Use exactly three sentences and add one soccer emoji.'*"  
* **Journal Brainstorming (10 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher**: "Open your Prompt Journals. Your task is to brainstorm and write down the ideas for your website sections in English."

**6\. Prompt Journal**

* **Format**: Structured paper blank.  
* **Action**: The student completes the first brainstorming session on paper, writing down the ideas for their website sections (e.g., "About Me", "My Hobbies") in English.

**7\. Gating Rules (Paper Checklist)** Because this lesson is "unplugged" (without computers), readiness is verified via a physical paper checklist by the teacher:  
Rule / ConditionStatus (Check)**Card Sort Complete**The pair successfully placed 15 cards into AI/Not AI/Unsure piles and participated in the discussion.**Agreement Signed**"Classroom AI Use Agreement" is signed using the student's class nickname (pseudonym), NOT their real name.**Mind Map Drawn**The paper mind map shows website structure using target English vocabulary (header, footer, navigation bar).**Journal Started**The paper Prompt Journal contains at least the first brainstormed ideas for website sections in English.  
**8\. Materials / Assets**

* **Assets Needed**:  
* Printed "AI or Not AI" card sets (15 situations per pair).  
* Printed "Classroom AI Use Agreement" sheets.  
* Paper templates for Mind Maps.  
* Structured paper blanks for the Prompt Journal.
