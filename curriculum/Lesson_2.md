**Lesson Spec Sheet: Lesson 2**  
**1\. Lesson Objective**

* **Content:** Students will learn the prompt formula (Role \+ Context \+ Task \+ Format) and apply iterative prompting to generate and edit the "About Me" and "My Hobbies" sections of their website.  
* **Language:** Students will critically edit AI-generated text to match their actual language level, practice specific target adjectives, and use the Present Perfect tense to describe personal experiences.  
* **AI-literacy:** Students will recognize and avoid the "lazy prompting" trap, understanding that AI is a tool that requires precise, iterative guidance rather than accepting its first generic output.

**2\. Target Vocabulary** *Note: The vocabulary is kept to 10 words to ensure a balanced cognitive load while focusing on the new prompting formula and editing tasks.*

1. Role  
2. Context  
3. Task  
4. Format  
5. Energetic  
6. Boring  
7. Creative  
8. Paragraph  
9. Hobby  
10. Experience

**3\. Grammar Focus** Present Perfect (mandatory linguistic constraint for About Me and the three hobby texts to prevent language fossilization and encourage complex sentence structures).  
**4\. Timing (90 minutes total)**

* **00:00 – 00:05 (5 min):** Introduction and reading the Driving Question aloud.  
* **00:05 – 00:20 (15 min):** The Prompt Formula (Role \+ Context \+ Task \+ Format) and defining "lazy prompting".  
* **00:20 – 00:40 (20 min):** "Terrible First Draft" Activity (We do) – Open Discussion.  
* **00:40 – 01:15 (35 min):** Generating & Editing "About Me" text with artificial linguistic constraints.  
* **01:15 – 01:30 (15 min):** Completing the Prompt Journal and Wrap-up.

**5\. Scenario**

* **Intro & Driving Question (5 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher:** "Welcome back\! Today we are working on the text for your website. But first, let's remember our main goal. Listen carefully: **How can I critically partner with Artificial Intelligence to build a digital portfolio that authentically represents who I am to the world?** Today, we focus on the text that authentically represents your **experience** and your **hobby**."  
* **The Prompt Formula & Lazy Prompting (15 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher:** "If you just say 'write about my hobby', the AI gives you a **boring** text. This is the 'lazy prompting' trap. To get a **creative** text, we use a prompt formula."  
* **Teacher:** "Remember, a prompt is a set of clear instructions you write to tell the AI exactly what to make or write for you. Our formula has four parts: **Role**, **Context**, **Task**, and **Format**."  
* *Example 1:*  
* **Teacher:** "Listen to this prompt. **Role**: A 12-year-old student. **Context**: I have played football for three years. **Task**: Write an **energetic** 'About Me' text. **Format**: One short **paragraph**."  
* *Example 2:*  
* **Teacher:** "Here is another. **Role**: A **creative** gamer. **Context**: I have built three Minecraft cities. **Task**: Write an introduction. **Format**: Two short **paragraphs**."  
* **"Terrible First Draft" Activity (20 min) \- Open Discussion:**  
* *Question Bank:* "Look at this bad prompt on the board: 'Write about my pet.' What is missing from our formula?", "How can we add a **Role** to this prompt?", "What **Format** should we ask for?".

* # "Terrible First Draft" — Board Example

* ## The lazy prompt that made this

* **"Write about my pet."**

* ## The bad AI draft (project this first)

* I have a pet. My pet is nice. I like my pet very much. My pet is fun to play with. I think pets are good for people. Pets make people happy. That is why I like my pet.

* ## Facilitation

* Ask the class: *"Is this energetic? Is this creative? What is missing?"* Expected answers: it's repetitive, it says nothing specific, we don't even know what animal it is. This is what "lazy prompting" produces — technically correct English, zero personality.

* ## Rebuilding it with the formula (do this together, live)

* **Role**: A 12-year-old dog owner  
* **Context**: I have had my dog Max for two years, and he loves chasing tennis balls in the park  
* **Task**: Write a short, fun paragraph about my pet  
* **Format**: One paragraph, energetic tone

* ## The good version (reveal after building the prompt together)

* I've had my dog Max for two years, and he's obsessed with tennis balls\! Every afternoon, we race to the park, and Max chases the ball faster than anyone else on the block. He's loud, he's silly, and he's the best hiking partner I could ask for.

* ## Why this works as a teaching example

* Uses Present Perfect naturally ("I've had... for two years") — ties directly to this lesson's grammar focus.  
* Concrete, specific, sensory details instead of generic statements — the exact contrast the activity is meant to teach.  
* Matches target vocabulary tone (*energetic*, not *boring*).  
* *Expected Child Answers:* "It needs to know what pet you have\!", "You can say 'Act as a dog lover'.", "Ask it to write one short **paragraph**.".  
* **Generating & Editing (35 min) \- Scripted:**  
* **Teacher:** "Now, open your platform. Write your first prompt for the 'About Me' section using our formula. Remember to use the Present Perfect tense, like 'I have played' or 'I have visited', to share your **experience**."  
* **Teacher:** "When the AI answers, do not just copy it\! Read it. Is it too **boring**? Are the words too difficult? You must edit it."  
* **Teacher:** "Our platform has a rule to stop lazy prompting: Every next prompt must be at least 3 words longer, and you must use at least 2 target words from today's lesson."

**Hobby completion:** Each of the three hobbies uses one RCTF prompt with Present Perfect, then Publish or manual Improve + reason (no second AI request). About Me requires two prompts with reviews, then Operator edit. Teacher checks meaning, not only the one-character edit.

**6\. Prompt Journal**

* **Format:** Digital table integrated into the platform or a structured paper blank.  
* **Action:** The student creates a comparative table for their text generation. They must log: 1\) The original AI output, 2\) The errors or overly complex words they found, and 3\) Their manually edited, simplified version. There must be a minimum of 2 justified iterations for the text block.
