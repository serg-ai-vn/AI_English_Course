# Activity: "AI or Not AI?" — Card Sort

## For the teacher

Print or write out the 15 situations below as individual cards (or read them aloud one by one if no printer is available). Students work in pairs. For each card, they place it into one of three piles: **AI**, **Not AI**, or **I don't know**.

Do not reveal the answer key before the sort. The goal is discussion, not a quiz — three of the cards are deliberately debatable, and the "I don't know" pile is meant to be used.

After sorting, go through each card together and ask: *"Why do you think this is/isn't AI?"* End with the class building a collective definition together (target: something close to *"AI is a computer system that finds patterns in data and uses them to make predictions"*).

## The 15 cards

1. YouTube or TikTok suggests a video you might like, based on videos you watched before.
2. A traffic light turns green after exactly 30 seconds, every single time.
3. A phone camera adds dog ears and a nose to your face in a photo.
4. A calculator shows "4" when you press "2 + 2 =".
5. Google Translate changes a sentence from Vietnamese into English.
6. A teacher reads your homework and writes "Good job!" on it.
7. Netflix suggests a new movie based on what you've watched before.
8. A GPS app finds the fastest way to school.
9. A chatbot answers your question about homework at 11pm.
10. An elevator door opens when you press the button.
11. An email app decides a message is spam and hides it automatically.
12. A robot vacuum learns the shape of your room and cleans it better every week.
13. A washing machine stops when the timer reaches 0:00.
14. A weather app predicts rain tomorrow using patterns from years of old weather data.
15. Spell-check underlines a word that might be spelled wrong.

## Answer key (for the teacher only)

| # | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI | Learns your pattern of watching, predicts what you'll like |
| 2 | Not AI | Fixed timer — no data, no pattern, no learning |
| 3 | AI | Recognizes the pattern of a face to place the filter |
| 4 | Not AI | Fixed rule of math — always the same answer |
| 5 | AI | Learned language patterns from huge amounts of text |
| 6 | Not AI | A human, not a computer system |
| 7 | AI | Same pattern-based recommendation as #1 |
| 8 | **Discuss** | Route-finding is often a fixed calculation, not learned patterns — many apps call it "smart" even when it isn't really AI. Good one for "I don't know." |
| 9 | AI | A chatbot generates responses using a trained model |
| 10 | Not AI | Simple mechanism, no data or prediction involved |
| 11 | AI | Learned to recognize patterns of spam vs. real mail |
| 12 | AI | Builds and improves a map of your room over time |
| 13 | Not AI | Fixed timer, same as #2 |
| 14 | **Discuss** | Real weather forecasting mixes classic statistics and AI — a great "it depends" case |
| 15 | **Discuss** | Older spell-checkers used fixed dictionaries (not AI); modern ones often use learned patterns (AI). Depends on the app! |

## Facilitation note

Cards #8, #14, and #15 are your best discussion tools — save extra time for them. The point isn't to get every card "right," it's for kids to leave with the idea: *not everything called "smart" is actually AI, and it's OK to say "I'm not sure, let's think about it."*
