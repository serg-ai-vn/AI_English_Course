**Lesson Spec Sheet: Lesson 4**  
*v2 — актуализирована 06.08.2026 под реализацию платформы (продуктовая спека: Scaffolding Removal для картинок хобби + тема через формулу R.C.T.F.). Отличия от v1: активность ручной «сборки» сайта убрана (тексты и аватары из L2/L3 попадают в слоты автоматически при Build site учителем); добавлены картинки хобби; тема выбирается по формуле промпта, а не свободным описанием настроения.*

**1. Lesson Objective**
* **Content**: Students will generate images for their three hobbies with decreasing scaffolding (publish the first acceptable image or improve it up to 6 attempts), then give their site a unique visual identity by choosing a theme through the R.C.T.F. prompt formula and comparing two options.
* **Language**: Students will acquire visual/layout and design vocabulary, and use comparative and superlative adjectives to compare and justify their theme choice.
* **AI-literacy**: Students will experience "Scaffolding Removal" — the teacher's support fades and the student decides when the AI output is good enough to publish. They direct AI within a bounded set of choices (mood words → theme presets), reinforcing that personalization still requires clear communication and evaluation, not a single click.

**2. Target Vocabulary**
*(Two banks, shown per-field in the UI:)*
* **Hobby images (bank: new/review):** `background`, `section`, `layout` (new); `concept art`, `close-up`, `volumetric lighting`, `avatar` (review from Lesson 3).
* **Theme (bank: new):** `theme`, `palette`, `mood`, `contrast`, `font`, `bold`, `soft`, `match`.

**3. Grammar Focus**
Comparative and superlative adjectives (e.g., "This palette is brighter than that one," "This is the boldest option") — emerges naturally from comparing theme options before choosing.

**4. Timing (90 minutes total)**
* **00:00 – 00:05 (5 min)**: Introduction and reading the Driving Question aloud.
* **00:05 – 00:30 (25 min)**: Hobby Images — "Scaffolding Removal" (You do, individually).
* **00:30 – 00:55 (25 min)**: Choosing Your Site's Theme with the R.C.T.F. formula (You do together).
* **00:55 – 01:15 (20 min)**: Comparing & Refining Themes (second option, open discussion + individual work).
* **01:15 – 01:30 (15 min)**: Prompt Journal completion and Wrap-up.

**5. Scenario**

**Intro & Driving Question (5 min) – Scripted:**
**Teacher**: "Welcome to Lesson 4! Today we finally put all our pieces together, and give your site its own personality. Let's remember our main goal for the course. Listen carefully: **How can I critically partner with Artificial Intelligence to build a digital portfolio that authentically represents who I am to the world?** Today, your site doesn't just say who you are — it *looks* like you too."

**Hobby Images – Scaffolding Removal (25 min) – Scripted:**
**Teacher**: "Your texts and your avatar are already on your site. But a website about hobbies without pictures of those hobbies? Not yet a website! Today you add three pictures — one for each hobby. The AI gives you a picture from your **prompt**. Use the same formula as in Lesson 3: **Subject**, **Style**, **Framing**, **Lighting**."

**Teacher**: "This time I will not force you to make three versions of everything. You are the **Operator** now. Look at the first picture. Is it good enough to publish? Great — publish it. Is something wrong with it? Then tell the AI why in English (at least 3 words), change at least one part of your formula, and try again. You have up to 6 attempts. The decision is yours."

*Example 1:* "Subject: a skateboard trick on a ramp. Style: concept art. Framing: dynamic low angle. Lighting: warm afternoon light." — first try, publish.
*Example 2 (improve):* reason "the board looks too small", new prompt changes Framing to "close-up of the board" → regenerate.
*Teacher note:* this is the "Scaffolding Removal" step: unlike Lesson 3 (mandatory 3 iterations, pros/cons, best/worst), here the student decides the quality bar. The 6-attempt cap keeps it bounded.

**Choosing Your Site's Theme (25 min) – Scripted:**
**Teacher**: "Your site has words and pictures now, but every site right now looks the same — same colors, same **font**. Today we fix that. Every website has a **theme**: a color **palette** and fonts that create a **mood**."

**Teacher**: "You don't pick colors one by one. Instead, you describe the **mood** you want in English, and the system shows you a matching theme. To describe it, we use our old friend — the prompt formula: **Role**, **Context**, **Task**, **Format**."

**Teacher (models on the projector):** "Role: You are a theme designer. Context: My website is about my love of music and dance. Task: Suggest a theme for my site. Format: A calm theme." — and the system shows me a first theme option.

*Example 1*: Mood word `calm` → Theme: Ocean Calm — soft blues, gentle font, and your picture gently comes together from little blue dots.
*Example 2*: Mood word `contrast` or `bold` → Theme: Comic Pop — bright colors, bold contrast, comic font.
*Example 3*: Mood word `energetic` → Theme: Comet Trail — light background, bold violet contrast, purple particles.

**Teacher**: "One rule: your mood word goes into the **Format** line. You can use one of these eight: **bold, soft, contrast, energetic, calm, playful, warm, cool**."

**Comparing & Refining Themes (20 min) – Scripted intro, then Open Discussion:**
**Teacher**: "Don't just accept the first theme! Ask for a second option — change the mood word in your Format line. Compare. Which one is **bolder**? Which one is **softer**? Which one **matches** you **better**?"

*Question Bank*: "Which theme is brighter — the first or the second?", "Does this **contrast** make the text easy to read?", "Which one feels more 'you'?"
*Expected Child Answers*: "The second one is calmer than the first.", "This one has better contrast, I can read it easily.", "I like the boldest one because I'm loud and I love games."

**Teacher**: "Once you've compared at least two themes, choose your favorite and write one sentence in English explaining why — use a comparing word like 'brighter', 'calmer' or 'the boldest', and connect it to your design: **theme, palette, contrast, font, bold, soft**."

**Prompt Journal & Wrap-up (15 min) – Scripted:**
**Teacher**: "Open your Prompt Journals. For each hobby picture, your journal already keeps the prompt and the reason you changed it. Add your theme story: the mood prompt, which theme you got, what the second option looked like, and why you chose your final one."

**Teacher**: "Before we finish — tell your partner one word to describe your theme: bold, soft, or somewhere in between?"

**Teacher**: "Fantastic work. Next time is our last lesson: we audit everything and you present your one-of-a-kind website to the world!"

**6. Prompt Journal**
* **Format**: Digital table integrated into the platform.
* **Hobby images (auto-logged per iteration):** the four-part prompt (Subject/Style/Framing/Lighting) + the change reason (≥3 words) for every regeneration. The published version is marked; attempts left shown.
* **Theme:** 1) the R.C.T.F. prompt (mood word in Format), 2) the theme it produced, 3) the second option's prompt + theme, 4) a justified final choice using at least one comparative/superlative adjective and one design word (theme/palette/contrast/font/bold/soft).

**7. Gating Rules (Platform Logic Table)**

**Hobby images (`ai-image-rapid`):**

| Rule / Condition | Action if Failed (System Prompt/Hint) |
|---|---|
| **Prompt Structure Check** — all four labels `Subject:`/`Style:`/`Framing:`/`Lighting:` present and non-empty | "Use the formula: Subject:, Style:, Framing:, Lighting:." |
| **Target Vocabulary Check** — ≥1 word from the bank (background/section/layout + review words) | "Try using today's visual words in your prompt: concept art, close-up, volumetric lighting, background, section, or layout." |
| **Change Reason Check** — regeneration requires a reason ≥3 words | "Please explain in English why you want to change this image before generating a new one." |
| **Structure Change Check** — new prompt differs in ≥1 formula field | "You are the Operator! Please change at least one visual detail (Subject / Style / Framing / Lighting) in your next prompt." |
| **Attempt Cap** — max 6 versions per image; publish required after the 6th | "You have used all co-pilot attempts. Choose the best result from your history or publish this final image!" |

**Theme (`theme-picker`):**

| Rule / Condition | Action if Failed (System Prompt/Hint) |
|---|---|
| **R.C.T.F. Structure Check** — all four labels present | "Use the formula with all four labels: Role: ... Context: ... Task: ... Format: ..." |
| **Context Check** — Context line describes the site in ≥3 words | "Describe what your website is about in the Context line (at least 3 words)." |
| **Mood Check** — one of 8 mood words (bold/soft/contrast/energetic/calm/playful/warm/cool) in the Format line | "Use a mood word in your Format line: bold, soft, contrast, energetic, calm, playful, warm, or cool." |
| **Minimum Comparison Check** — a second prompt is required; if it maps to the same theme, a soft warning (not a block) | "Try a different mood - you got the same theme twice. Change the Format line." |
| **Choice Check** — student must choose Theme 1 or Theme 2 | "Choose Theme 1 or Theme 2 to compare before publishing." |
| **Theme Justification Log Check** — ≥10 chars/3 words + comparative/superlative ("brighter", "the boldest", "than") + ≥1 design word (theme/palette/contrast/font/bold/soft) | "Please write a full, meaningful sentence in English to justify your design choice as an Operator." |

*Build note: the Comparative/Superlative check is a lightweight pattern match (whitelist of -er/-est forms, "more"/"most", or "than"), not full grammar parsing — same category as the Present Perfect rule in Lesson 2. The mood word maps to one of 6 presets (soft→Ocean Calm, contrast→Comic Pop, etc.).*
